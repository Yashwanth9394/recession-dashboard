Tier 2 · Scheduled signals — strongest for timing

Structural time bombs

Not live feeds — contracts, maturity schedules, and historical lag statistics that already fix a future window, the same way a 2005 teaser-rate mortgage had its reset date written into the loan on day one. The question each one answers: what pain is already locked into today's system but hasn't shown up in today's data yet? Updated by hand when the underlying report updates, each stamped with its own date.

Commercial real estate maturity wall

$875–936B due in 2026

Loans written in 2015–2021 at ~3–4% rates carry fixed maturity dates — not "pay whenever." When the date arrives, the owner must refinance at today's ~6.2% or sell. If a building's rent doesn't cover the new payment, default follows. A further $652B matures in 2027.

Source: Mortgage Bankers Association / S&P Global · as of Aug 2026

Corporate debt maturity wall

$268.8B B-and-below rated debt due 2028

The weakest-rated borrowers hit their wall first — 2028, concentrated in the US — while the aggregate speculative-grade peak has actually been pushed out to 2031 (it was 2029 in April, 2028 in January) as 2025's active refinancing wave moved borrowers ahead of schedule. That's its own signal: the system is deferring this one, not resolving it.

Source: S&P Global Ratings, Industry Credit Outlook 2026

Auto loan negative equity

30% of trade-ins underwater

Average shortfall $7,200, up 42% in five years. 90.2% of these loans stretch to 72+ months — the term extension defers the reckoning to the next trade-in or refinance, the same deferred-pain shape as a teaser-rate reset, just via loan length instead of interest rate.

Source: Edmunds Q1 2026 Insights Report

Monetary policy lag

18–29 months, hike to full effect

Housing reacts to rate changes within months; business investment, hiring, and consumer spending arrive last. Whatever the Fed did a year and a half to two and a half years ago is still working through the system now, regardless of what rates are doing today.

Source: Fed transmission-lag meta-analysis; Atlanta Fed

Leverage amplifiers

Record hedge fund gross leverage

Not a scheduled event — a buffer check. Hedge fund gross notional leverage sits at near all-time highs and large life insurers remain in the upper quartile of their historical leverage range. These don't cause a shock, but they determine how hard one gets amplified if it arrives. Counterweight: the Fed still describes banks overall as well-capitalized.

Source: Federal Reserve Financial Stability Report, May 2026

Household buffer check

3.00% auto flow-to-serious-delinquency

Up from 2.93% a year earlier; credit card flow held flat near 6.97%. Not an explosion by itself — but it tells us how much cushion households have left when the next scheduled shock (the CRE or corporate wall above) actually lands.

Source: NY Fed Household Debt and Credit Report, Q2 2026 (Aug 2026)
